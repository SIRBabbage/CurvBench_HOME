Overview

Existing graph and relational-learning benchmarks often compress heterogeneous datasets into a single flat leaderboard. CURVBENCH asks a sharper question: do models generalize across intrinsic geometries, or only within the regimes that match their inductive biases?

🧭

Geometry-aware evaluation

Datasets are partitioned into near-zero, positive, and negative curvature regimes using curvature statistics.

📊

Regime-conditioned rankings

Model comparisons are reported within each geometric regime, reducing misleading conclusions from global averages.

🧪

Broad model coverage

The benchmark spans Euclidean GNNs, hyperbolic models, mixed-curvature methods, adaptive Riemannian models, and GFMs.

🔁

Reproducible protocol

Code, splits, evaluation tools, and diagnostic scripts are designed to support rigorous relational-learning assessment.

Method

CURVBENCH treats graphs as finite metric spaces and summarizes their intrinsic geometry through node-level midpoint curvature residuals.

Curvature residual

For a center node m, neighbor pair {b,c}, and anchor node a, the midpoint residual measures deviation from Euclidean midpoint geometry. Graph-level curvature is summarized by mean curvature and skewness, which together reveal both average geometry and tail-driven asymmetry.

mean curvature curvature skewness partial-order ranking within/cross regime gap
ξG(a,b,c;m) = [ dG(a,m)2 + 1/4 dG(b,c)2
  − 1/2( dG(a,b)2 + dG(a,c)2 ) ] / [ 2dG(a,m) ]

κ̄(G) = mean node curvature
γκ(G) = curvature skewness

Evaluation lens

Rather than asking which model is globally best, CURVBENCH asks which inductive bias is reliable under each intrinsic geometry.

Regime Representative datasets Geometric signal Diagnostic value
● Near-zero Cora, Citeseer, PubMed Balanced curvature profile Tests whether flat aggregation and spectral filtering are sufficient.
● Positive Cornell, Airport, Actor Compact or clustered geometry Reveals when node attributes and local clustering dominate.
● Negative Disease, Telecom, CS_Phds Hierarchical or tree-like geometry Evaluates whether non-Euclidean or adaptive models reduce mismatch.
● Table-derived Carcinogenesis, Hepatitis, PTE, Toxicology, F1 Near-zero mean with strong curvature tails Exposes specialist–robustness trade-offs hidden by average curvature.

Key Findings

Curvature is not merely a post-hoc descriptor: it reorganizes model preferences, top-performing families, and GFM transfer behavior.

Observation 1 — coherent regime orders

Datasets within the same curvature regime exhibit much more similar top-model preferences than datasets across regimes.

Observation 2 — inductive biases shift

Euclidean methods dominate near-zero graphs, while mixed and adaptive Riemannian methods become strongest on negative-curvature graphs.

Observation 3 — GFMs remain geometry-conditioned

Few-shot GFM performance does not collapse into a single universal ranking; leading methods differ across regimes.

Observation 4 — labels reveal scalability frontiers

Additional labels help citation-like graphs more than structurally demanding regimes, exposing geometry-limited transfer.
